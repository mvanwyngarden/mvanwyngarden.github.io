### Welcome!

Hi! I am a 2nd year PhD student and [NSF Graduate Research Fellow](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/grfp-nsf-graduate-research-fellowship-program) at the University of Arizona Steward Observatory. I work with Dr. Eiichi Egami to explore the dust obscured universe with JWST and ALMA. I am currently leading an ALMA 2mm survey of the 60 lensing fields observed by the JWST Cycle 4 treasury program [VENUS](https://jwst-venus.github.io) to study dusty star forming galaxies in the early universe. 
<br>
<br>
Before moving to Tucson, I was a [Fulbright Student](https://us.fulbrightonline.org/about/fulbright-us-student-program) at the [Canadian Insitute for Theoretical Astrophysics](https://www.cita.utoronto.ca/) at the University of Toronto. Under the supervision of Professor [Maya Fishbach](https://mayafishbach.me/), I studied how to use gravitational waves to learn about cosmology. In particular, I worked to improve methodologies in the “dark siren” method, a way of combining data from gravitational wave detections and galaxy catalogs to measure the local expansion rate of the Universe.
<br>
<br>
I completed my Bachelor’s in Astronomy & Physics at Boston University. During my undergrad, my research spanned multiple topics and disciplines. I studied the pulsations of white dwarf stars observed by NASA’s Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite. As an REU student at the [Center for Astrophysics | Harvard & Smithsonian](https://www.cfa.harvard.edu/opportunities/graduate-undergraduate-programs/reu-summer-intern-program#:~:text=News-,Overview,full%20duration%20of%20the%20program/), I wrote software to test different formation and evolution mechanisms of small exoplanets. I even spent six months at CERN using machine learning to quantify the efficiency of proposed detector upgrades for ATLAS.
